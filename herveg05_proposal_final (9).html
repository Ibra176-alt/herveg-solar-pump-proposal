Executive Summary

The ask. The idea.
The case.

HERVeg.05 will help 100 women farmers in the Njombe Region acquire solar-powered water pumps through a mobile savings and subsidy model. Each farmer saves 75% of the pump cost. We cover the remaining 25%. The pump is registered in her name. She incurs no debt.

With a pump, a farmer moves from one rain-fed growing season per year to two or three. That means more food for her family, more vegetables to sell, and income she can count on during dry spells.

The Problem

Tanzania has the water.
Women just can't reach it.

Tanzania has enough water and sunlight to feed its people year-round. But 70% of its workforce depends on agriculture, and almost all of that agriculture depends on rain. When rain is late, short, or absent, crops fail, income vanishes, and families go hungry.

Common Questions
How will my money be used? ↓
60% goes directly to pump subsidies ($9,125). 20% to a one-time app upgrade ($3,000). 10% to village agent training ($1,500). 10% to operations and M&E ($1,500). Zero new hires — existing staff absorb the pilot. Every cent is accounted for above.
How do I know this works? ↓
Independent research backs it. Duke University studied 750+ households — solar pump users doubled their irrigated plot share. IWMI found income gains of up to $5,262/hectare on dry-season crops. HERVeg.05's own cohorts show 34% yield increases and 40% drop in food insecurity. Our pilot targets are deliberately conservative against this evidence.
What happens after 12 months? ↓
HERVeg.05 does not walk away. The 35–40 pump owners stay in our active farmer network. Village agents continue quarterly visits as part of existing duties — no extra cost. Pump warranties cover years 1–2. We publish the full evaluation, seek a second cohort of 100 farmers, and improve the subsidy ratio from 75/25 toward a fully self-sustaining revolving fund.
Is there a risk women lose the pump to male family members? ↓
We take this seriously. Every pump is legally registered in the woman's name. Village agents conduct household onboarding with both the farmer and her household to build shared understanding before the pump arrives. Quarterly visits monitor ownership status. If pressure is reported, the programme intervenes directly with household mediation.
